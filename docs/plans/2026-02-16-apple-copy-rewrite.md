# Echo Landing Page -- Apple-Style Copy Rewrite

## Apple's Core Writing Principles (as applied to Echo)

After studying Apple's marketing pages and copywriting analysis, these are the principles that matter most for Echo:

1. **One idea per headline.** Apple never sells two things at once. "Light. Years ahead." does one job. "Pro cameras. Pro display. Pro performance." does one job three ways. Each headline earns its own moment.

2. **Benefits first, features second.** Apple says "Tap to add your voice to any conversation" -- not "voice messaging feature included." The user's experience leads; the technical capability follows. For Echo: "Your voice stays on your Mac" beats "local AI transcription engine."

3. **Contrast and juxtaposition.** "Less to carry, more to love." "The good news: you can work anywhere. The bad news: you can work anywhere." Tension between opposites creates memorability. For Echo: the free vs. paid, local vs. cloud, simple vs. powerful tensions are goldmines.

4. **Rhythm through fragments.** Short sentences. Fragments even. Then a longer one to let the reader breathe. Apple breaks grammar rules deliberately -- periods after fragments create pace and punch. "Any kind of file. On all your devices." isn't a sentence. It's better than one.

5. **Wit without trying.** Apple's humor is dry, confident, and never forced. "Fumble, bumble, and tumble resistant." It acknowledges reality without overselling. Echo's current copy does this well in places -- the testimonials section subhead is a good example.

6. **Direct address.** "You" and "your" everywhere. Apple writes to one person, not an audience. "It's your personal portable powerhouse." The copy feels like a conversation, not a broadcast.

7. **Sensory and concrete language.** Apple avoids abstraction. They don't say "high quality display" -- they say "every pixel is powered by you." For Echo: don't say "smart formatting" -- describe what actually happens.

8. **Strategic repetition.** "Pro cameras. Pro display. Pro performance." Repetition of structure creates rhythm and emphasis. It's not redundancy -- it's reinforcement.

9. **Confidence without arrogance.** Apple states facts. They don't plead. "The most powerful MacBook Pro ever" isn't arrogant because it's simply true. Echo can do the same: "Free. Forever. No exceptions."

10. **The unsaid is as powerful as the said.** Apple leaves space. Not every feature needs explaining. Sometimes a three-word headline with a screenshot is everything. Let the product breathe.

## Current vs Proposed Copy

### Hero Section

**Current:**
- Headline: "Your voice, perfectly transcribed."
- Subhead: "Local AI. Cloud engines. Smart formatting. Context-aware profiles. One free Mac app."
- CTA: "Learn more"

**Proposed:**
- Headline: "Think it. Say it. Done."
- Subhead: "Voice-to-text for Mac. Local AI, cloud engines, and smart profiles -- in one free app that respects your privacy."
- CTA: "See how it works"

**Alt headline options:**
- "Your voice. Your Mac. Your rules."
- "Speak freely."
- "The voice app your Mac has been missing."

**Why:** The current headline is functional but passive -- "perfectly transcribed" describes a result, not an experience. Apple leads with what the *user* does and feels, not what the product does. "Think it. Say it. Done." is three fragments that mirror the actual user flow (the three-step "how it works" section below) and create rhythmic momentum. The subhead condenses the same information but leads with the category ("Voice-to-text for Mac") before listing differentiators, giving the reader context before details. The current subhead reads like a feature checklist -- Apple would make it flow as a sentence.

## General Notes

### What's Already Working Well
- **The pain section is the strongest copy on the page.** It's specific, honest, and conversational. The naming of competitors with exact prices is bold and effective. This section needs the least work.
- **The overall structure is sound.** Problem, solution, features, proof, comparison, CTA -- this is a well-paced landing page. The section order doesn't need changing.
- **The voice is consistent.** The current copy has a clear personality -- direct, slightly informal, developer-aware without being developer-exclusive. This voice should be preserved, not replaced.
- **The testimonials are well-written.** They sound like real people, not marketing personas. Keep the conversational, slightly rambling quality -- it builds authenticity.

### Overall Tone Observations
- **The current copy leans informational; Apple copy leans experiential.** The biggest shift is from telling people what Echo *has* to showing them what Echo *does for them*. "Multiple on-device models like Whisper and Parakeet in different sizes" is informational. "On-device models in multiple sizes -- from lightweight to studio-grade" is experiential.
- **Feature labels are doing too much naming and not enough selling.** "Adaptive Awareness" and "Smart Vocabulary" are product names that mean nothing to a first-time visitor until they read the description. The headlines below each label need to do the selling instantly. Apple uses feature names too (Dynamic Island, MagSafe) but their headlines immediately explain the benefit: "A magical new way to interact with iPhone."
- **The page could use more emotional contrast.** Apple's best pages make you feel something -- usually a combination of desire and reassurance. Echo's page is competent and credible, but it could be more *exciting*. The product genuinely is remarkable (free, open-source, offline-capable, with features that match paid alternatives) -- the copy should convey that same sense of "wait, really?"

### Structural Suggestions
- **Consider adding a single-line "thesis" between the hero and pain section.** Something like: "Voice typing that actually works. Free. Private. No strings." -- a one-sentence summary for scanners who won't read every section.
- **The comparison section could move earlier.** For a free product competing against paid alternatives, the comparison table is one of the strongest conversion tools. Consider placing it before or immediately after the feature showcase, not near the bottom.
- **The bento grid (Four Pillars) and the feature showcase cover similar ground.** Consider whether both are needed, or whether one could be cut to tighten the page. If both stay, they should feel distinct -- right now they cover the same features (Adaptive Awareness, AI pipeline, providers) with slightly different framing.

### Pacing Note
The page currently has 9+ distinct sections. Apple's product pages are long, but each section has breathing room and strong visual breaks. Make sure the spacing between sections is generous -- cramped copy undermines the feeling of confidence and calm that Apple achieves. White space is copy too.
